How to Store Kaspa (KAS) Safely

Most losses in self-custody wallets come from a handful of avoidable mistakes, not from Kaspa's protocol itself. Here's what actually matters.

1. Back up your seed phrase offline

When you create a self-custody wallet, you're given a seed phrase — write it down on paper (or engrave it on metal) and store it somewhere offline. Never store it as a plain text file, screenshot, or note synced to the cloud; any of those can be accessed if that account or device is ever compromised.

2. Never enter your seed phrase into a website or app that asks for it

A legitimate wallet only ever asks for your seed phrase once, during setup or recovery, inside the wallet itself. If a website, browser extension, "support" agent, or DM asks you to type or paste your seed phrase anywhere else, it's a phishing attempt — full stop.

3. Double-check addresses and domains before sending

Malware on a compromised device can silently swap a copied address for an attacker's address. Verify the first and last few characters of an address before confirming a send, especially for large amounts. Using a KNS domain for frequent recipients reduces this risk for repeat transfers.

4. Keep your device and browser up to date

Since a browser-based self-custody wallet encrypts and stores keys locally, the security of your device matters. Keep your operating system and browser updated, and avoid installing browser extensions from untrusted sources on the same profile you use for crypto.

5. Consider what "backup" really means for you

If your seed phrase and your device are both lost at the same time, funds are not recoverable — not by you, and not by the wallet provider, because a true self-custody design means no one else ever held a copy of your keys. Store your backup somewhere separate from the device itself (a different physical location), not just a different folder on the same computer.

6. Understand what you're approving

If you connect your wallet to a dApp, review exactly what you're signing before approving it. A wallet should show you the transaction details, not just ask for a blind approval.
