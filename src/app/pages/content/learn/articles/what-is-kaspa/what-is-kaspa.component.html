What Is Kaspa (KAS)?

Kaspa is a proof-of-work cryptocurrency built on a BlockDAG instead of a single blockchain, using a consensus protocol called GHOSTDAG to confirm many blocks per second.

The problem Kaspa's design solves

Traditional proof-of-work blockchains like Bitcoin produce blocks one at a time on a single chain. If two miners find a valid block at nearly the same moment, only one can be kept — the other is discarded as an "orphan," and its work is wasted. That trade-off is part of why chains like Bitcoin keep block times relatively slow: producing blocks faster would mean orphaning more of them.

GHOSTDAG: keeping blocks instead of discarding them

Kaspa uses a protocol called GHOSTDAG (a generalization of the GHOST rule), which arranges blocks into a BlockDAG — a directed acyclic graph — rather than a single chain. Blocks that would be orphaned on a traditional blockchain are instead included in the DAG and ordered by the protocol, so near-simultaneous blocks from different miners can both count instead of one being wasted. That's what lets Kaspa safely produce blocks much more frequently than a single-chain design.

Block rate and confirmation speed

Following Kaspa's 2025 "Crescendo" network upgrade, the mainnet produces 10 blocks per second, up from 1 block per second at launch. Because new blocks arrive so frequently, transactions reach practical confirmation much faster than on typical single-chain proof-of-work networks.

Fair launch, no premine

Kaspa launched in 2021 based on research by Yonatan Sompolinsky (one of the original authors of the GHOST protocol) and collaborators. It had no premine, no founder allocation, and no ICO — all KAS in circulation was mined the same way, through proof-of-work.

What you can do with Kaspa today

Beyond sending and receiving native KAS, the Kaspa ecosystem supports fungible tokens (KRC-20), NFTs (KRC-721), and human-readable addresses through KNS domains. You can manage all of these from a self-custody wallet like KaspaCom Wallet, where your keys stay on your own device.
